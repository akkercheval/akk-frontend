## Backpack

A sturdy, cared-for woodland pack: structured canvas body, leather acorn-shaped flap, drawstring closure, two leather shoulder straps, leather hanging loop, oak-leaf repair patch, and an actual acorn-shaped cookie cutter tied to the hanging loop. It is practical and handmade in character, not tactical, synthetic, or magical.

Pack FRONT means the outward oak-leaf-patch face; BACK means the face resting against Gypsa. LEFT/RIGHT are the wearer's sides. Camera turns do not rename attachments.

| Flap | 0.80H long: cap 0.32H (40%), nut 0.48H (60%) |

| Stick-carry strap width | Approximately 0.12H; two straps |

The flap's cap is broad and rounded with diamond-lattice quilting and a gently scalloped cap/nut boundary. Diamond-lattice quilting appears only on the cap. The nut portion has a smooth, unquilted leather surface and stays full for a substantial distance before tapering to a soft point. No acorn stem. The canvas mouth gathers beneath the flap.

Both shoulder straps attach to the **back face**, each top-left to bottom-left or top-right to bottom-right. They do not cross or attach to the pack's side faces. Their loops carry the shoulders naturally; occlusion determines how much is visible. The leather hanging loop is centered above/behind the flap hinge.

The enlarged oak-leaf patch sits on the canvas front below the flap, roughly one-third of the visible body-front height, with careful stitching. The acorn-shaped cookie cutter is a functional baking utensil: an open-centered metal band with the depth needed to cut dough, tied by a short knotted cord to the hanging loop. Preserve its hollow construction and visible band depth where the viewing angle permits; do not reduce it to a wire outline, decorative charm, or solid medallion. It is a keepsake from her grandmother.

The pack remains between shoulder and tail root, leaving clearance for natural tail volume. It does not compress the root or force the neutral tail into a new curve.

## Walking stick

An undecorated found oak branch: gently irregular and sturdy but refined. Her walking stick has a single comfortable grip bend and an asymmetric Y-fork. Avoid a straight dowel, wand, club, or wizard staff. Keep knots and grain restrained. Exact knot inventory varies across legacy sources and is not finalized here.

In the held reference arrangement, the grip bend curves toward Gypsa; the longer fork arm is body-side and the shorter arm points away. The shorter arm is approximately 60% of the longer arm's length. Preserve the branch's asymmetry when rotating it; do not redesign it to fit the hand.

### Stowed carry

One full-length stick is secured along the pack's **wearer's-right side**, corresponding to Gypsa's anatomical right when the backpack is worn, by two plain leather straps, upper and lower. This attachment side is fixed across camera views; do not move the straps to whichever side is hidden in a particular image. No holster, boot, toggle, or crosswise mounting. The stick may extend beyond the pack. For Squirrel Mode it travels with the pack's orientation; it does not remain screen-vertical independently of the body.

Gypsa stows the stick when preparing to run on all fours and keeps it secured while doing so. Otherwise she holds it or sets it aside, for example propped against a tree. Stowed carry is not her default upright walking or standing arrangement. An upright transition pose may show her securing the stick before dropping to all fours.

For the website's usual leftward departure composition, show her left side nearer the camera so the right-side stowed stick sits on the far side. Natural occlusion can hide some of it, but the attachment side does not guarantee complete concealment. Turning or changing the camera can reveal it; maintain the same physical mounting. Verify clearance from limbs, ground, head, and tail in the final running construction.

## Bandana

A simple square cloth folded corner-to-corner into a right triangle and tied with a small **square knot at the front of the throat**. The folded long edge wraps the neck, and two tied ends drape together over the upper chest.  The triangle point drapes over the back.  When worn, the Backpack shoulder straps pass over the band at the shoulders, and the top of the backpack partially covers the triangle point.

It is soft everyday cloth, not a stiff collar, bow tie, cravat, cape, or hood. It sits at the neck base without hiding or changing the chin/jaw. Exact triangle drop, end lengths, and fold-line drawings remain to be validated. Keep the solid oak-leaf green specified in the rendering standard; seasonal patterns are deferred.

## Occlusion and validation

- Hidden flap, patch, cookie cutter, or straps are not automatically missing. Do not move them onto the chest to expose them in front view.
- Validate hand contact with the grip, strap attachments, bandana wrap, pack envelope, and tail clearance in appropriate views.
- Do not substitute a shaded historical prop render for the final flat-color standard.
- Final detail plates must establish cap scallop/lattice treatment, leaf outline/stitches, and cord and hardware treatment, and demonstrate the established right-side carry without changing the structure.
