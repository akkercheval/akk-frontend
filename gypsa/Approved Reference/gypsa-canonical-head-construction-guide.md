# Gypsa's Canonical Head Construction Guide

## Purpose

This guide consolidates the approved construction of Gypsa's head into
one working reference. It is intended to freeze approved structural
components while future dependent features, expressions, rotations, and
full-body integrations are tested.

Gypsa is a stylized Eastern Gray Squirrel. Her visual language favors
rounded forms, expressive eyes, natural asymmetry, organic authenticity,
and a silhouette that remains recognizable when rotated, simplified, or
scaled.

## Canonical Views

Use three master views for head construction:

1.  **Front**
2.  **3/4 Left**
3.  **Profile Left**

These are orthographic construction references rather than new
interpretations. When a dependent feature is explored, approved geometry
beneath it should remain unchanged.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 1. Basic Head Shape

### Approved character

-   Soft, rounded squirrel cranium.
-   Head is broad enough to support full cheeks without becoming
    spherical or childlike.
-   Cheeks remain full and warm, but underlying facial structure is
    still suggested.
-   The forehead flows gently into the crown.
-   In profile, preserve substantial rounded cranial volume behind the
    ear.
-   Do not narrow the rear skull or allow the back contour to fall away
    abruptly.
-   The head should remain recognizably the same solid form in all three
    canonical rotations.

### Construction rule

**The skull is the foundation. Hair/fur, ears, muzzle, and expression
must conform to it rather than redefining it.**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 2. Muzzle + Cheeks

### Approved construction

-   Small, soft nose.
-   Muzzle projects forward enough to communicate curiosity and
    engagement.
-   Fuller lower jaw preserves warmth and kindness.
-   Chin remains gentle rather than sharply defined.
-   Cheeks are full but not excessively spherical.
-   A subtle planar transition marks where muzzle meets face in 3/4
    view.

### Core principle

**The muzzle should seem to inflate from the skull rather than attach to
it.**

Think of a balloon gently expanding beneath a blanket: the surrounding
facial surface flows over the muzzle without a hard glued-on boundary.

### Rotation behavior

-   **Front:** bilateral muzzle volume remains balanced beneath the
    nose.
-   **3/4:** the near muzzle gains visible projection while the far side
    compresses naturally.
-   **Profile:** muzzle projects clearly forward while remaining
    integrated into cheek and jaw.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 3. Eyes

### Approved character

-   Large, expressive, almond-shaped without overwhelming the surrounding face.
-   Soft, friendly construction appropriate to a curious young-adult
    squirrel.
-   Upper lid is clearly defined.
-   Lower lid remains softer.
-   Eye size and shape should not change to strengthen an expression.
-   Surrounding facial structure balances the eyes so Gypsa does not
    read as a small child.

### Default expression relationship

Gypsa's default attentive expression uses slightly widened eyes from
**interest**, not surprise. Her gaze often sits just past the viewer as
though she has noticed something nearby.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 4. Organic Fur Brows

### Approved construction

-   Brows are composed of natural directional fur strokes rather than
    groomed human-style eyebrow shapes.
-   Light, delicate, and slightly irregular.
-   Follow the eye's natural gesture without becoming smooth cosmetic
    arcs.
-   Preserve the directional-fur character when the brow deforms for
    expression.
-   Brow motion may change the overall gesture, but should not make the
    brows appear styled.

### Expression principle

Gypsa's expressions are cumulative: brows contribute a small amount
alongside eyes, mouth, ears, and head pose rather than carrying the
entire emotion alone.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 5. Ears

### Canonical identity

Gypsa has **squirrel ears, not cat ears and not rabbit ears**.

-   Moderately sized.
-   Soft taper.
-   Rounded upper contour.
-   Gentle inward curve.
-   Broad, natural base.
-   Attached **low on the sides of the skull**, not perched on top.
-   Ears are attached to the sides of the head and face each side, NOT the front.

### Front view

-   Gypsa faces directly forward.
-   Ears are symmetrical in height and placement.
-   Only the **front edge** of each ear is visible; the interior should
    not face the viewer.
-   Ear bases attach low on the sides and emerge naturally.
-   Tips rise only slightly above the skull.
-   Rear edges slope backward and outward from the head.

### 3/4 view

-   Near ear preserves canonical size, contour, and low attachment.
-   Far ear sits naturally behind the near ear in rotation.
-   Do not shorten the ear or migrate its base toward the crown.

### Profile view

-   Front/root attachment is visible just above the eye line.
-   Rear/root attachment is **lower and farther back**.
-   Preserve the rounded cranium behind and beneath the ear.
-   Ear length must remain consistent with the other views.

### Expression behavior

Ear expression comes from **rotation plus modest natural flexion**,
never resizing. Root attachment remains anatomically anchored. An
excited ear must not become longer; a concentrating ear may bend gently
backward without flattening into fear or aggression.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 6. Soft Layered Sweep --- Forehead & Crown Fur

**Authority (updated 2026-10-09):** [Gypsa Crown Fur Specification](Gypsa%20Crown%20Fur%20Specification.md) v1.0 governs this feature. Origin is centered between the ears at the rear crown. Sweep is clockwise **viewed directly from the front**, initially toward image-right / Gypsa's left ear, then curving inward to the longest tip on the facial midline. The neutral tip height is halfway vertically between eye-opening top and the lowest **center-facing** eyelash point for each eye. Project the same volume into other views; do not recenter by screen-space eye averages. Older references' “Gypsa's right” sweep wording is superseded.

### Approved direction

Gypsa does not have human hair. She has an area of naturally longer
forehead/crown fur whose length and direction create a recognizable soft
sweep.

### Construction

-   A small group of primary layered tufts begins over the upper
    forehead.
-   Front tufts are longest.
-   Fur flows asymmetrically across the crown.
-   Individual locks vary subtly in length and direction.
-   Crown volume stays shallow enough that the underlying rounded skull
    remains readable.
-   Longer fur becomes progressively shorter toward the rear.
-   It eventually becomes indistinguishable from ordinary head fur.

### Critical profile rule

**There is no visible place where the "hair ends."**

The transition is:

**longer front fur → layered crown fur → progressively shorter rear fur
→ normal coat**

The back of the head must retain the canonical rounded silhouette. Do
not create a raised forelock followed by an abrupt drop.

### Front-view refinement

-   Keep the sweep soft rather than messy.
-   Avoid an excess of prominent spikes on Gypsa's right side.
-   Maintain gentle natural asymmetry.
-   The lowest front tuft may approach the Organic Fur Brow but should
    not overlap it in the neutral canonical head.
-   The longest central tip may descend **between** the brows to the specified eye/lash midpoint. Keep both brows fully visible; do not require all tips to remain above brow-top height.
-   A small additional tuft may add fullness on the opposite side, but
    it must belong to the same flow rather than appearing as a separate
    decorative spike.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 7. Combined Canonical Head

A successful complete head combines all approved elements without
allowing any one component to distort another:

-   canonical rounded skull;
-   integrated muzzle and full cheeks;
-   approved expressive eyes;
-   Organic Fur Brows;
-   canonical low-mounted squirrel ears;
-   Soft Layered Sweep transitioning naturally into ordinary head fur.

### Front

The head remains broad and rounded. Ears emerge from the sides and are
seen largely edge-on. The sweep provides soft asymmetry while keeping
both brows readable.

### 3/4 Left

The muzzle projects naturally, cheek structure remains present, the near
ear retains its low attachment, and the layered fur reveals how it
travels over the crown rather than behaving like a flat fringe.

### Profile Left

This is the strongest structural validation view. The muzzle must
project from the skull, both ear-root relationships must make anatomical
sense, the cranium must remain full and rounded behind the ear, and the
crown fur must taper gradually into the normal coat without changing the
skull.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 8. Locked Relationships

These relationships should be treated as constraints during future
rendering:

-   Ear bases remain low on the sides of the skull.
-   Ear size and physical shape remain constant across neutral
    rotations.
-   Front-view ears do not turn their interiors toward the viewer.
-   Profile front/root attachment sits just above the eye line.
-   Profile rear/root attachment sits lower and farther back.
-   Rounded cranial volume continues behind the ear.
-   Cheeks remain full without reverting to an overly childlike
    spherical face.
-   Muzzle inflates naturally from the skull.
-   Eye size remains stable.
-   Organic Fur Brows remain fur-like even when expressive.
-   Forehead/crown fur never obscures the neutral brow.
-   Crown fur follows the skull and gradually merges into ordinary coat.
-   Hair/fur does not repair or redefine an incorrect skull silhouette.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 9. Validation Rules

1.  **Validate one variable at a time.**
2.  **Freeze approved components while testing dependent features.**
3.  **Test in isolation before context.**
4.  **Use orthographic validation before expressive validation.**
5.  **When something looks wrong, identify the responsible component
    before changing approved geometry.**
6.  **A passed validation is evidence to stop redesigning that
    component.**
7.  **Profile views are mandatory when attachment or rear-skull
    relationships are involved.**

The canonical head should rotate, simplify, and scale without losing
Gypsa's identity.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 10. Expression Compatibility

The approved head must support the established core expression system:

-   Neutral / Calm
-   Inquisitive
-   Concentrating
-   Puzzled
-   Amused
-   Delighted / Excited
-   Concerned / Empathetic
-   Determined / Problem-Solving

Expression should be built cumulatively from small coordinated changes
in brows, eyes, mouth, ears, and head position. Structural head geometry
remains Gypsa.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 11. Production Intent

This guide is a construction reference, not permission to reinterpret
Gypsa from scratch.

When creating future art:

> **Use the canonical head as the geometry. Add behavior to Gypsa; do
> not redesign Gypsa in order to create behavior.**

The goal is a repeatable head that can serve as a stable foundation for
expressions, animation, SVG construction, full-body integration, and
future storytelling.
