## Skull and muzzle

### Muzzle volume — accepted wording

Gypsa's muzzle is a broad, softly rounded volume that projects forward from the face and blends continuously into the cheeks. Its upper bridge descends gently toward a compact nose. The lower jaw remains full, with a soft chin beneath the mouth. Preserve this depth and fullness across camera views and expressions; do not flatten the muzzle into a facial marking, narrow it into a pointed snout, or remove the lower-jaw volume when changing the smile.

The muzzle inflates smoothly from the skull, like volume beneath a continuous surface, rather than forming a glued-on oval. The warm fur marking does not define the entire anatomical muzzle. A closed-mouth smile is an expression choice, not a permanent structural requirement.

### Skull, cheeks, and brow-to-forehead transition

The overall skull shape and cheek fullness in the archived `Gypsa head correction no hair.png` are the user's preferred starting appearance. Preserve its squirrel-like rounded cranium, substantial volume behind and beneath the ears, and full but structured near cheek. Do not broaden it into a spherical baby-like head or narrow the rear skull.

**Refinement requested:** round and smooth the underlying brow area slightly. Use the early `Gypsa Canonical Muzzle.png` study as guidance for the gentle rising transition from the muzzle end, along the bridge between the eyes, past the brow region, and into the upper skull. Move toward that softly continuous curve without tracing it exactly, flattening the muzzle's projection, or replacing the preferred skull and cheek volume. This concerns the underlying head surface/contour, not the shape of the eyebrow fur strokes. Subtle changes of plane can remain; avoid an abrupt notch or angular break at the bridge/brow transition.

The skull is the foundation. Ears, crown fur, and expression conform to it. Do not use crown volume to conceal an incorrect forehead or rear-skull contour.

## Eyes and brows

Eyes are large, calm, almond-shaped “Quiet Observer” eyes with defined upper lids and softer lower lids. Surrounding facial structure balances them so Gypsa reads as a young adult. Do not enlarge or round the underlying eye design to intensify emotion. Lid opening and gaze can change with expression.

**Canonical iris color: solid dark brown #533923**, slightly darker than backpack leather brown. Each iris is a uniform flat fill without texture, mottling, radial streaks, gradients, or lighter bands. Distinguish iris from black pupil, light sclera, and white catchlight; a single dark “eye color” token cannot describe the whole eye. Rendering Standard owns the color tokens.

**Pupils:** retain a distinct solid black oval in each iris. For the current calm head reference, the requested pupil span is approximately 75% of the iris width and height, following the iris perspective and gaze, with a visible brown border. This is a linear span, not 75% of its area. Preserve the white catchlight over the pupil. Do not treat the generated study as proof of an exact measured ratio or as a rule preventing natural pupil changes in future explicitly directed expressions.

**Canonical eyelashes:** three distinct upper-lid lash details per eye, grouped toward the outer corner and sweeping gently outward/upward. Use short, tapered, curved shapes integrated with the upper lid; keep them readable as three details without creating a dense fringe. The far set is foreshortened with its eye rather than copied at the near eye's screen-space size. No lower-lid lashes. Preserve eye size, almond shape, and gaze when adding or posing lashes.

**Updated brow direction:** each eyebrow is a cohesive filled shape with a soft, bushy fur outline. It sits between a perfectly smooth cosmetic arc and a loose collection of brush lines. Directional fur is expressed chiefly through restrained contour variation; do not fragment the brow into separate hairs or make it a heavy jagged block. Brow movement contributes alongside eyes, mouth, ears, and head pose without losing the recognizable filled fur mass. This supersedes the earlier stroke-only brow rule.

**Accepted eyebrow definition:** surround each filled brow with the dark inward outline specified in the Rendering Standard. Follow its existing fur contour without enlarging the brow's outer silhouette or changing its placement and expression. The user accepts the outlined-brow appearance in the 2026-10-28 eyebrow study; this does not approve the study's crown, ears, or all other features.

**Current eyebrow shape — canonical, 2026-10-29:** a slim, gently arched, cohesive band of warm brown fur, fuller through the central portion and tapering toward its ends, with small restrained tufts along the contour. Preserve the current reduced thickness and soft bushy edge; do not return to the earlier heavy brows, perfectly smooth painted arcs, or disconnected brush strokes. The 25% reduction was relative to the earlier study's brow thickness, not an additional reduction to apply on every render and not a change to the 75%-weight inward outline. The latest `Gypsa Head Black Pupils 75 Percent Study 20261029.png` supplies the accepted brow-shape example only; its brighter brown is not a palette authority. Use the canonical muzzle/belly brown for the fill. Brows remain separate from the lids with visible gray fur between them and retain their coherent shape through expression.

**User-confirmed eye-area pattern:** gray fur surrounds the eyes, with light-brown filled brows using the face's light brown. Preserve gray separation between each brow and the upper lid. Do not add a contrasting eye ring, mask, or lower-eye crescent. Exact palette values and repeatable facial marking boundaries belong to the Rendering Standard.

The brown-gray muzzle/face fur boundary uses the fine outline specified in the Rendering Standard. Preserve a clearly visible gray gap between that line and both eye outlines, including their corners; they must not touch or merge. This is separation within the existing gray eye surround, not an additional colored eye marking. Preserve eye placement and muzzle volume.

**Below-eye clearance — canonical:** the gray strip must remain continuous beneath both lower eye contours wherever they approach the muzzle marking, including the foreshortened far eye and lower corners. It must still read at the intended display size. Move the local fur-color boundary if necessary; do not move or shrink an eye, flatten the muzzle, or insert a new pale crescent to manufacture clearance. No fixed pixel width is established yet. A study in which the outlines meet fails this requirement even if its other features are accepted.

## Ears: fixed construction, variable attention

**Inner-ear color — accepted 2026-10-30:** the visible inner surface uses the same flat warm brown as the muzzle, belly, and eyebrows, as specified in Rendering Standard. Do not restore the older pale tan. Outer ear fur and the ear backs remain gray. This changes color only, not ear shape, placement, rotation, or visibility.

**User-confirmed correction:** both roots remain mounted low on the sides of the skull in every pose and orientation. Attentive rotation does not move a root higher, backward, or onto the crown. A changing visible contour is not a changing anatomical attachment.

The Soft Taper ear is moderate in size, broad-based, with a soft rounded upper contour and gentle inward curvature. Preserve believable thickness and volume. Avoid sharp triangular cat-like silhouettes, exaggerated pointed tufts, rabbit-like length, or circular mouse-like discs.

- **True neutral:** ears face either side of the head. A direct frontal view shows chiefly their front edges.
- **Typical behavior:** ears rotate slightly forward or toward the object of attention. Some interior can become visible; its visibility is not itself an error.
- **Viewer-engaged:** modest forward rotation is permitted while keeping the same low roots, size, and underlying form.
- **Other camera views:** project the actual ear orientation into the camera. Profile or three-quarter view does not automatically require hiding the interior.
- Expression permits rotation and modest natural flexion, never resizing or remounting.

In profile, the front attachment is near/just above the eye line; the rear attachment is lower and farther back. Rounded cranial volume continues behind and beneath the ear. Tips rise modestly above the skull. Exact root/tip coordinates and rotation angles await annotation; no numerical angle is prescribed. ## Crown: Soft Layered Sweep

The crown is naturally longer squirrel fur forming one broad, softly layered sweep with a natural curl. It is part of her coat, not a wig or separate human hair. It originates **just behind Gypsa's anatomical left ear** and flows across the crown toward the forehead, turning gently downward and inward at its longest front end. In the current left-facing three-quarter view, this origin is behind the near ear at image-right. Keep the origin attached to the same anatomical side through camera changes; do not mirror the character or relocate the origin to suit the composition.

Use broad overlapping fur groups with rounded bodies and gently tapered ends. Their contours should join into a relatively smooth overall sweep. The groups nearest the left ear must be long enough to blend into the main mass, rather than forming a row of short upright spikes. Subordinate internal lines describe that shared flow; they must not divide the crown into many narrow, disconnected locks. Blend the rear into ordinary coat without a separate hairline or abrupt drop.

Preserve the fuller tuft developed from the user's older shape reference: broader and larger than that small reference tuft, with restrained height above the skull. The front end should read as a soft curl rather than a projecting spike or a tight, narrow hook. Keep the rounded skull and forehead evident beneath it. Avoid a mohawk, tall crest, spiky quiff, sawtooth fringe, or flattened wedge.

Retain the shortened front-curl reach established during the current studies and the user's subsequent manual cleanup. Both eyebrows remain visible, and the curl must not extend deeply down between the eyes. Do not move the eyebrows, eyes, ears, or skull to accommodate the fur. The curl's exact anatomical endpoint and dimensions remain to be annotated on the validated master; no numerical tip formula or fixed screen-space center is prescribed. A change of expression must not pull the crown tip down with the eyelids or lashes.

For development, the user's manually edited head baseline is the authority for the hair details they retained or removed; do not restore deleted spikes or eye-adjacent hair lines from earlier generations. The older tuft image supplies overall shape guidance, not scale, ear design, or a complete head specification. These source roles and superseded constructions are recorded in the progress tracker. No existing study is a fully validated crown turnaround.

## Expression system

These behavioral descriptions are accepted by the user. Their visual implementation must be validated on the completed head. Anatomy remains constant.

| Expression | Coordinated behavior |
| --- | --- |
| Neutral / Calm | Level resting head, balanced relaxed brows, soft eyes, gentle closed smile; true-neutral ears face sideways |
| Inquisitive | Attentive eyes, subtly unequal brows, slight head tilt; ears may attend toward the discovery; small closed smile |
| Concentrating | Focused gaze, modest lowered brow/lid gesture, head slightly inclined toward the task |
| Puzzled | Mild brow asymmetry and questioning head gesture; thoughtful uncertainty rather than distress |
| Amused | Relaxed eyes and brows, small playful smile, subtle head turn/tilt |
| Delighted | Brighter eye opening and lifted expression; smile may open without redesigning muzzle/jaw |
| Concerned | Soft attentive eyes, modest inward brow gesture and head incline; empathy rather than alarm |
| Determined | Focused gaze, restrained firm brow/mouth gesture, head directed toward the task; no aggressive redesign |

The older approximately 7-degree inquisitive tilt is a behavioral guide, not compulsory neutral geometry. A calm greeting can use attentive ears without adopting an exaggerated inquisitive expression.
