# Gypsa Tail Render Standard Prompt  

Create a clean reference drawing titled “Gypsa: Canonical Straight Tail Fur Envelope.”  

Draw ONLY Gypsa's tail fur envelope and its structural centerline on a clean white background. Do not draw Gypsa's body, head, limbs, anatomy, reference grid, dimension arrows, technical legend, or decorative elements.  

## Canonical Scale  
Gypsa's canonical measurement unit is H (Head Height).  

1H = 196 pixels in the source Gypsa Profile View.  

The accepted Profile View structural tail centerline measures approximately 800 pixels along its curve. Therefore:  
    800 px/196 px=4.0816H  

Define Gypsa's canonical structural tail length as: 4.08H  

All dimensions below are proportional to this canonical scale. The drawing may be rendered at any convenient resolution, but these proportions must remain unchanged.  

## Structural Centerline  

Draw a perfectly straight horizontal structural centerline from left to right.  
Left endpoint = **ROOT**  
Right endpoint = **STRUCTURAL TIP**  
Structural centerline length = **4.08H**  
Draw the centerline as an orange dashed line.  
Do not curve the centerline.  

The structural tip is an **internal anatomical/construction endpoint**. It is not the visible fur tip.  

The fur silhouette must pass smoothly across the structural-tip location without any shoulder, flattening, kink, seam, abrupt narrowing, or other visible indication that the centerline ends there.  

## Neutral Fur Construction  

Treat the visible fur envelope as being formed by individual fur spikes/groups originating along the structural centerline.  

The entire tail has been gently **combed from root toward tip**.  

Therefore every fur spike has a distal bias. **No fur spike may lean backward toward the root**.  

Through the mature region:  
- Standard fur-spike length = approximately 0.408H  
- Standard neutral fur angle = approximately 45° relative to the local centerline  

Upper fur projects upward **and toward the tip**.  

Lower fur projects downward **and toward the tip**.  

Preserve the soft, flowing, slightly irregular visual character of stylized eastern gray squirrel fur. Do not turn the envelope into a mechanically uniform sawtooth pattern. The measurements govern the underlying construction; subtle organic variation should soften the visible silhouette.  

## Root Transition  

Fur must **not** begin at full mature length.  

At the root:  
- Initial fur-spike length = approximately 0.102H  
- This equals 25% of standard mature fur length.  
- Root fur already leans toward the distal tip.  

During the first **0.408H** of structural tail length, progressively increase fur length from approximately **0.102H to 0.408H**.

The transition must be continuous and natural:  
    **short body-like fur → progressively longer transitional fur → mature tail fur**  

Do not create a narrow stalk, hourglass pinch, sudden flare, or immediately fluffy root.  

## Mature Region  

From approximately **0.408H through 3.57H from the root**, maintain approximately the standard **0.408H** fur-spike length.  

Maintain an approximately **45° distal fur orientation** and a reasonably consistent apparent tail thickness.  

Allow controlled organic variation in individual fur groups without creating major random changes in overall volume.  

## Continuous Distal Taper  

Begin the visible distal taper at approximately:  

**3.57H from the root**  

This is **87.5% of structural tail length**.  

From this point through the visible fur tip, create **one continuous uninterrupted taper**.

During this taper:  
1. Progressively reduce outward fur projection.  
2. Progressively reduce fur angle from approximately 45° toward 0°.  
3. Increasingly align terminal fur with the distal direction of the centerline.  
4. Progressively narrow both sides of the silhouette toward the longitudinal axis.  

The structural centerline ends at **4.08H**, but this must cause **absolutely no visible change** in the taper.

Do not terminate, restart, abruptly shorten, or suddenly reorient the fur at the structural endpoint.  

## Furred Tip  

Fur continues approximately **0.408H beyond the structural tip**.

Therefore the approximate visible fur tip lies:  
    **4.49H from the root**  

No new structural centerline exists beyond 4.08H. The visible extension is created by distal fur anchored at or before the structural endpoint.  

As the structural tip approaches, these terminal fur groups become increasingly aligned with the centerline and extend beyond it.  

The final central fur group approaches **0° relative to the centerline** and forms a **soft, pointed or nearly pointed visible tip**.

Do not create a rounded, blunt, bulbous, clipped, or flattened tip.  

## Critical Canonical Rules  
- Structural tail length = **4.08H**  
- Standard mature fur length = **0.408H**  
- Root fur length = **0.102H**  
- Root transition length = **0.408H**  
- Mature neutral fur angle ≈ **45° distally**  
- Distal taper begins = **3.57H from root / 87.5% of structural length**  
- Structural tip = **4.08H**  
- Visible fur tip ≈ **4.49H from root**  
- Terminal fur angle progressively approaches **0°**  
- Every fur group flows **root → tip**  
- No fur group deliberately leans toward the root  
- No expression-dependent fluffiness  
- No color or marking development  

This drawing establishes Gypsa's **canonical neutral tail fur construction**. Preserve the quantitative relationships above rather than inventing new proportions.  

## For Non-Straight Renders  
At every point, determine the local root→tip tangent of the expression centerline. Neutral fur projects approximately 45° from that local tangent, while always retaining a positive distal component.
