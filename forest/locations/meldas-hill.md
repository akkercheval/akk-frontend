# Melda's Hill — LotRO

*Website pages: LotRO (`/lotro`)*

*Design direction: a comfortable hillside dwelling whose sheltered veranda expresses Melda's hospitality, craftsmanship, and lifelong habit of keeping watch.*

Melda's Hill sits farther from Gypsa's home, at the woodland edge overlooking a small wooded valley. The familiar Friendly Forest path climbs gently, the trees thin, and the visitor arrives at a low home tucked sideways into a grassy slope. Its broad, quiet silhouette follows the hill's contours. Beside the round wooden entrance, a sheltered veranda opens toward the valley and the approaching path.

The home brings together Melda's hobbit and elven heritage. The earth-sheltered rooms, round door, garden, and domestic comfort express her hobbit side. The veranda's graceful timberwork, careful proportions, and relationship to the landscape express her elven side. Each influence has a practical role in the home.

This is an original home that could belong in Middle-earth, rather than a reproduction of Bag End, Rivendell, or another recognizable Tolkien location. Familiar Friendly Forest vegetation — oak, clover, ferns, acorns, and woodland wildflowers — keeps the transition ambiguous. Did we enter Middle-earth, or simply an older, quieter corner of the Friendly Forest?

## Melda and Her Home

Melda is Gypsa's great-aunt: quiet, patient, observant, protective, and welcoming in an unhurried way. Her home should feel like somewhere she has cared for over many years, with time to tend herbs, mend belongings, remember old journeys, and listen to a visitor's story.

The veranda is a frequently used part of her home whenever she is there. It gives her a comfortable place to sit outdoors in shelter, watch the approaching path, and look across the valley. Its openness expresses her watchfulness without making the home feel defensive or remote.

Melda grows herbs both for seasoning food and for medicinal use, along with lettuce and mushrooms. She also drinks tea. Enjoying a cup on the veranda is a natural part of her quiet time at home, whether she is alone or sharing the bench with a visitor. No particular tea variety or herb species is established yet.

The environment can be designed before Melda's character artwork is complete. No character is present in the canonical background images. Her habits and hospitality are evident in the arrangement and care of the place. When she eventually appears, the visitor should think: "Oh. Of course this is her house."

Future additions should feel earned through new stories rather than arbitrary redesigns, following the Character Bible. Melda's Hill remains the name of this location.

## Architecture and Permanent Features

### Hillside Home

- The home is low and broad, partially set into the slope, with a grass-covered roof that follows the hill's natural contours.
- A round, weathered wooden door sits within a modest surround of carefully fitted stone. Muted green woodwork provides a restrained color accent.
- Deep-set windows bring daylight into the rooms behind the facade. Their frames and proportions should feel related to the entrance and veranda.
- Weathered gray stone and graceful timber form the visible architecture. Everything is well maintained, with signs of long use and occasional repair.
- A modest chimney rises above the grassy roof. A small curl of smoke can suggest a hearth in use.
- The house belongs to the slope. It should not resemble a freestanding cottage with a mound piled over it.

### Veranda and Terrace

The roofed veranda sits beside the entrance and opens toward the valley. Slender timber posts and gently curved braces support a practical, weatherproof roof. Its craftsmanship is quietly elegant, with flowing structural lines rather than elaborate applied ornament. It should feel domestic in scale and comfortably usable in rain or snow.

An old, well-cared-for wooden bench occupies a sheltered place with a clear view of the approaching path. There is room beside Melda for a visitor. The bench is a permanent feature and one of the home's strongest personal details.

The veranda meets a modest stone terrace that follows the slope. Where the terrace meets falling ground, a low stone edge provides a believable boundary without blocking the outlook. The entrance, bench, and veranda must form one connected, usable space.

### Garden and Approach

- The familiar path of flat, irregular stones climbs gently from the foreground to the entrance and terrace.
- Small stone-edged beds of culinary and medicinal herbs, with a modest patch of lettuce, follow the terrace, close enough to tend and harvest easily. The garden is useful, carefully maintained, and modest in extent.
- A small, deliberately tended mushroom-growing area occupies a shaded, sheltered garden edge. It should read as cultivation rather than scattered decorative toadstools. Its permanent location remains the same across the seasons; visible growth can change with the season.
- Meadow grasses and familiar wildflowers grow beyond the tended beds. Ferns and moss occupy shaded edges and stone joints.
- Firewood is neatly stacked in a sheltered place beside the house, clear of the entrance and seating.
- Temporary household objects should be few and suggest actual use.
- Trees frame the setting without enclosing the entire view. The valley remains a quiet presence beyond the home.

Every visible object should have a reason to be there. Leave room to sit, walk, work, and watch; the veranda is a lived-in space rather than a display of charming props.

## Distinction from Gypsa's Hollow

The difference should read from the landscape and architectural silhouette before the viewer notices small details.

| Element | Gypsa's Hollow | Melda's Hill |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dominant form | Massive vertical oak trunk and embracing roots | Low, gently sweeping hillside and horizontal veranda |
| Setting | Enclosed woodland shelter | Woodland edge with an open valley outlook |
| Construction | Natural hollows adapted into rooms and openings | Carefully fitted stonework and graceful timber integrated into the slope |
| Outdoor living | Small root porch and close vegetable garden | Sheltered bench for tea, stone terrace, culinary and medicinal herbs, lettuce, and a shaded mushroom-growing area |
| Mood | Intimate, richly textured, warmly busy | Spacious, composed, quietly welcoming |

Avoid a dominant foreground tree trunk, an entrance nested among enormous roots, or a dense canopy that hides the outlook. Trees remain part of Melda's setting, but the hill and veranda establish the home's identity.

## Rendering and Composition

Use the same warm, detailed watercolor treatment as the existing Friendly Forest backgrounds: textured natural materials, believable vegetation, seasonally appropriate light, and softer, slightly bluer detail in the distance. The landscape backgrounds are the style authority here; the mascot's flat-color character production rules do not govern these paintings.

Weathered gray stone, muted green woodwork, and open daylight give Melda's home its own balance within the shared palette. Warmth gathers around the doorway and windows. Preserve a welcoming atmosphere throughout the year.

Use the same exterior viewpoint for all four seasons. A moderately close, three-quarter establishing view should show both the sheltered entrance and the veranda's open relationship to the landscape.

- **Foreground:** the rising stone path, meadow edges, and a portion of the herb garden lead the eye toward the home.
- **Middle ground:** the round doorway, recognizable veranda structure, old bench, and terrace form the principal visual group. The grassy slope remains clearly readable around and above them.
- **Background:** a small wooded valley recedes beyond the terrace, framed by trees. It adds depth without overpowering the home or becoming a grand panoramic spectacle.
- **Upper area:** keep sky, distant foliage, and hillside relatively calm where the website header and content may overlap them. Essential identifying features should remain visible below or around the content area.
- **Mobile-safe core:** keep the doorway, enough veranda to recognize its sheltered structure, and the bench close enough together that a vertical crop can preserve the home's identity. The wider desktop view can reveal more of the valley. Confirm the actual crop and page overlay before finalizing the composition.

## Seasons

**Production order:** Begin with Summer. Once its exterior image is approved, use it as the structural reference for Fall, Winter, and Spring.

**Seasonal transformation rule:** Preserve the approved Summer camera position, hillside shape, architecture, door and windows, veranda posts and braces, bench, terrace, paths, garden beds, mushroom-growing area, trees, and valley geography across all four images. Change foliage, weather, light, temporary objects, and evidence of current activity. The home does not rebuild or redecorate itself each season.

### Fall

The surrounding woodland turns gold and russet, and the meadow grasses begin to dry. Fallen leaves gather along the terrace edges while the approach remains tended. A few small bundles of harvested herbs hang to dry beneath the veranda roof, clear of the seating. Warm windows and a little chimney smoke suggest comfort within. The bench remains ready for an unhurried afternoon outdoors.

### Winter

Snow traces the grassy roof, veranda roof, exposed curved braces, and bare branches. The approach and terrace have been cleared, with a light new dusting showing the continuing weather. The bench remains sheltered beneath the veranda roof, and the firewood stays dry beside the house. Warm windows and chimney smoke give the home a reassuring presence against the quiet valley. Keep the veranda visibly usable rather than burying its seating in snow.

### Spring

A gentle rain darkens the path and terrace stones. Trees begin to bud, the herb beds show fresh growth, and familiar spring flowers appear along the meadow and woodland edges. The bench remains dry beneath the veranda roof, offering a place to listen to the rain and look across the valley. Soft, overcast daylight keeps the scene fresh and hopeful.

### Summer

The culinary and medicinal herbs flourish alongside a modest patch of leafy lettuce. A small crop of cultivated mushrooms is visible in the shaded growing area. These are practical parts of the garden and remain subordinate to the home and veranda. Meadow grasses and wildflowers soften the terrace edges, and the surrounding trees are in full leaf.

The veranda offers leafy shade while the valley remains open to view. Light falls across the approach and stonework in a way that makes the sheltered bench inviting. A cup of tea rests securely within reach of the bench, suggesting Melda has only stepped inside for a moment. Keep the seating available and the scene quiet, with only a few signs of recent use.
