# Melda's Hill — LotRO

*Website pages: LotRO (`/lotro`)*

The hobbit-hole idea gives the LotRO area a strong enough identity to function as a destination in its own right without suddenly making the visitor feel they've left the website. It sits farther from Gypsa's home.

The Friendly Forest path climbs gently, trees thin, and suddenly we arrive at a grassy hill tucked against the woodland edge. Set into it is a round wooden door.

Not *Bag End*. Not a reproduction of any recognizable Tolkien location. Rather, it should use the broad visual language of a comfortable hole-dwelling fantasy home: rounded doorway, natural materials, deep-set windows, garden, chimney, little stone path.

Deliberately retain Friendly Forest vegetation around it — oak, clover, ferns, acorns, familiar wildflowers — so the transition is ambiguous. Did we enter Middle-earth? Or is this simply a strange corner of the Friendly Forest? Exactly.

## Melda

If this will eventually be Great-Aunt Melda's home, we can design the environment now as though she already lives there even before we design her. There might be a carefully tended herb garden, an old bench beside the door, a walking stick resting somewhere, stacked firewood, perhaps a kettle implied through a little curl of chimney smoke.

We don't have to explain any of it. When Melda eventually appears, the visitor should think: "Oh. Of course this is her house."

That follows the Character Bible's rule that future additions should feel earned through new stories rather than arbitrary redesigns. This is therefore a canonical named location — Melda's Hill, unless a better name emerges later.
