# Gypsa's Hollow — About Me / Gypsa / Crochet / Cats

*Website pages: About (`/about`), Crochet (`/crochet`), Cats (`/cats`), and the Gypsa section (`/gypsa`)*

Canonical seasonal viewpoints: Summer and Fall use the exterior view. Winter and Spring use the interior view. The exterior and interior depict the same physical home and must remain spatially consistent with one another.

The visible window, entrance location, major roots, and surrounding terrain should correspond logically between exterior and interior views.

## Exterior

Gypsa lives in a very old, broad oak tree deep in the Friendly Forest. Its roots form natural terraces around the base. A well-worn path of flat, irregular river stones approaches the tree. The path ends at a large flat stone step and a gently flattened old root that serves as a tiny natural porch. There's a handmade welcome mat in front of the door. The home entrance is a natural hollow that has been carefully adapted rather than carved into a perfect architectural doorway, and the porch appears to be a gently flattened old root.

Gypsa's door is slightly irregular and rounded at the top, crafted to fit the existing hole in the tree trunk rather than the hollow being reshaped to fit the door. There is an oak leaf-shaped sign hanging on the door. On one side of the sign, she's painted the words "Gypsa is away." When she's home, the sign is flipped to, "Come on in!"

There is a visible window set into another natural opening: roughly rectangular, but shaped to fit the existing hole in the bark. Around the tree, instead of grass, is thick, dark green moss. There's also a garden bed. When Gypsa is away, there are handmade curtains covering the window.

You can't see it, but behind the tree, there's a small storage shed for her gardening tools, and a door leading down to her root cellar.

### Image Composition

- **Tree trunk:** dominant and slightly left-of-center, filling much of the vertical space.
- **Canopy/top branches:** allowed to extend upward into the area that will be partly hidden by the header.
- **Window:** mid-left or center-left, high enough that it may be partly overlapped by the content box, but still at least partially visible.
- **Garden:** lower-left to center-left, clearly visible beneath the content box.
- **Door and porch:** on the right third of the image, low enough to remain visible below the content box and not get clipped by the header. It should not touch the extreme right edge of the image.
- **Path:** entering from lower center or lower left and leading toward the door.

Compared to Acorn Crossing, Gypsa's Hollow should show:
- less distant horizon
- more tree mass
- more enclosure by surrounding forest
- more domestic evidence: path, garden, mat, door sign, watering can, hoe

## Interior

The walls of the room are shaped by the hollow itself rather than being a conventional rectangular room. The room is warmly lit by the active stove and a few beeswax candles, while cool winter daylight enters through the window. Shelves and cabinets fit into natural curves of the walls. The floor is simply the wood of the tree, sanded smooth, and sealed with protectant.

There's a small wood-burning cook stove set against the curved wall on the right side of the composition, opposite the window. The stove pipe runs upward and disappears into the wall/ceiling area, implying a chimney through the tree.

Separating the living space from the kitchen is a wooden counter with two wooden barstools. Below the window is a small, flat pillow for her cat. The visitor sees the same window as they saw from outside, though the curtains are open so that you can see her garden, part of the path, and trees from the Forest. There are two small handmade scrap-cloth rugs, one at the door and one in front of the stove.

**When entering:**

- Right of the door: coat hooks, shoe area, carved book nooks
- Left of the door: large comfortable chair with quilt

**After stepping in and turning left:**
- Window area / living space on the left, with the chair closest to the door, between the door and the window.
- Stove on the right
- Counter/bar dividing kitchen and living space
- Two wooden bar stools on the living-room side

**Visible items**

**The room does not redecorate itself every season. Gypsa's current activities change within the same room.**

## Seasons

**Summer**
In the summer, Gypsa is away on an adventure. The visitor sees a well-tended garden with growing vegetables, a watering can, and a hoe beside her door. The curtains over the window are a bright, sunny yellow.

In the summer, everything should feel:
- warm
- lived-in
- green
- shaded
- cozy rather than expansive

**Fall**
In the fall, Gypsa is away visiting friends and family. The garden has been harvested, the tree's leaves are changing colors and starting to fall, and a basket of vegetables sits beside the door. The curtains are made of a heavy cloth, woven with the same colors as the leaves on the trees.

**Winter**
In the winter, Gypsa is home. This time of year, the visitor comes inside, where they see snow accumulating outside the window. There's a basket beside the chair with yarn and a crochet hook. In winter, the usual quilt is replaced by a crocheted blanket draped over the back of the chair. The curtains are dark green, open, and tied to each side with silver-colored ribbons.

**Spring**
In the spring, there is a gardening book on the small table beside the chair, and the basket of yarn is gone. The crocheted blanket from the winter has been replaced with a lighter quilt.

The window is slightly open to let in the fresh air but not enough to let the rain inside. The curtains are made of a light, flowery print, and are tied back with yellow ribbons. The snow has been replaced with rain, the trees are still bare, but their leaves are just starting to bud out. The garden is clean and freshly planted, with only a few tiny plants starting to appear.

The towel hanging on the side of stove has been replaced with one which has a daffodil on it, and the pitcher on the bar is now filled with daffodils.

*The room is the same cozy home, but winter hibernation has given way to damp, hopeful early-spring energy.*
