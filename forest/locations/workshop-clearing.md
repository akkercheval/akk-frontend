# The Workshop Clearing — Cookie Cutters / Web Projects

*Website pages: Cookie Cutters (`/cookie-cutters`) and Web Projects (`/projects`)*

*There's a clearing in the Forest not far from Gypsa's Hollow, where a huge, ancient oak tree used to live. Long ago, when the tree was struck by lightning and died, the residents decided to honor the tree by turning it into the center of a new place to meet and work together.*

The Workshop Clearing occupies a naturally open, sunny clearing beneath immense mature oak trees. Everything looks handmade, organic, well maintained, and accumulated gradually over generations rather than constructed as a single building. The ground has been worn smooth over time. Moss grows under tables and between stations where there's less foot traffic. There are flat, irregularly shaped and different colored stones placed along the paths between areas where foot traffic is most common.

Nothing in the Workshop Clearing was designed to look quaint. Everything exists because someone needed it, repaired it, improved it, or adapted it over many years. Its charm comes from accumulated usefulness.

## Features

- **Center**: This is the old lightning-struck stump and the visual and historical heart of the clearing. The trunk has been cut horizontally to form a broad, flat work surface. Its bark remains around the outside, while the top has been sanded smooth from decades of use. Drawers have been carefully fitted into old natural cavities in the stump rather than arranged like modern cabinetry to store tools and materials. Several sturdy stools, made from the fallen tree, surround it. This is a genuine multi-purpose workbench: carving, repairing, sorting cookie cutters, sewing or crochet preparation, sketching, food preparation, and communal projects can all happen here. While the stump has been well cared for, and many drawers have been added over the years, it is still immediately recognizable as a stump.

  The Center stump should feel massive and broad enough that several residents could comfortably work around it at once. It should feel broader and heavier than an ordinary table, closer to the rooted base of a giant oak than to a carved wooden island. It must read first as the rooted base of a huge ancient tree, and only second as a work surface.

- **Cooking Area**: There is a substantial communal outdoor kitchen directly behind the old tree stump work surface on the opposite side from the entry path. Two large old-fashioned wood-burning cook stoves stand beneath a deep timber shelter, with a generous wooden preparation counter between them.

  Beside the stoves is a practical sink basin and a hand pump for water. This is not a modern sink with a faucet. It is a simple wash basin with a separate old-fashioned hand water pump beside or just behind it. The basin itself is ceramic and resting on a wooden cabinet. The hand pump should be clearly mechanical and old-fashioned: a vertical pump body, a curved spout, and a long hand lever. There should be no modern faucet, no gooseneck tap, no paired hot/cold handles, no integrated kitchen sink fixture, and no modern plumbing appearance.

  On the other side of the sink basin is a large, fully closing storage cabinet which holds pots, pans, baking dishes, utensils, mixing bowls, and other communal cooking equipment. The Cooking Area is an open-sided shelter, not a room or building. It has no front wall and should remain visually connected to the rest of the clearing. Its ground has been completely covered with flat, close-fitting stones of various sizes, shapes, and colors.

- **Cookie Cutter Cabinet**: To the left of the cooking area and between the cooking area and the social area stands a large wooden cabinet. This cabinet was intricately designed, built from wood taken from the original fallen tree. It is a full height cabinet, half as wide as it is tall, and equally as deep as it is wide. The bottom 2/3 of the cabinet is divided into drawers, four drawers across, but each drawer short: just tall enough to comfortably store a single, shallow-handled cookie cutter. Each drawer has a shape on it in the center representing the category of cookie cutters stored within. All drawer category symbols should be simple carved silhouettes, not labels or written words. The top 1/3 of the cabinet has two doors which open out into a hanging display of several special and large cookie cutters.

  The many drawers of the Cookie Cutter Cabinet should remain visibly functional storage; do not exaggerate their carved category symbols into large decorative motifs or signage.

- **Perimeter Work Stations**: There are three sheltered work stations around the perimeter of the clearing to the right side of the Cooking Area. These work stations are all covered with a simple, bark-covered roof on four sturdy posts. There is a wooden wall created from slats at the back of each work station, and the roof is higher at the front which is closest to the Center than at the back. These stations were added over time rather than constructed all at once, though the center station was created first, from the wood of the old oak tree. Each has a bench or other work surface, seating, shelves or peg storage, and enough roof to keep residents dry during a light rain. Each work station has a wooden slat floor. These floors were built on top of a series of small logs, creating a single step up to the platform. There are small gaps between the slats, just enough for any rain or spilled water to flow through without being far enough apart to become a hazard.

  **Exactly three separate work stations must be visible. Do not combine, merge, omit, or substitute any of them. Each must have its own separate roof, four-post structure, raised wooden platform, and distinct work area. All three roofs must be simultaneously visible in the establishing view.**  

  The work stations are working shelters, not market stalls or display booths. Do not fill the back walls with decorative merchandise-like rows of jars and objects. Storage should be practical and somewhat sparse, with most tools and materials put away.  

  **Station 1, Messy Work**: Closest to the kitchen area is a work station which is best suited for messy work. Against the wall is a cabinet with a sink basin and a hand water pump. Similar to the sink basin in the Cooking Area, this is not a modern sink with a faucet. It is a simple wash basin with a separate old-fashioned hand water pump beside it. The shallow sink basin is carved directly into a large slab of stone which is resting atop a wooden cabinet. The hand pump should be clearly mechanical and old-fashioned: a vertical pump body, a curved spout, and a long hand lever. There should be no modern faucet, no gooseneck tap, no paired hot/cold handles, no integrated kitchen sink fixture, and no modern plumbing appearance.

  **Station 2, General Purpose**: The center work station has a clean, simple design with a broad work surface and serves as a general purpose work area. This station has shelves attached to the back wall containing tools and a simple cabinet where materials are stored. In front of the cabinet is an open work table with two stools.

  **Station 3, Fine Handwork**: The work station farthest from the Cooking Area was designed for fine handwork. Its work station was built with many drawers and doors on its front and back. There are hooks on either side of the work station, and the back wall is covered in shelves. This station has two cushioned stools.

  There's a wooden bucket hanging on the wall above the cabinet. In front of this is a large round table with a smooth top. There are two chairs sitting on one side of the table.

  While unique and specialized, none of the work stations should be labeled for only one craft. All three should look old, repaired, useful, and lovingly maintained rather than ruined or ramshackle.

- **Social area**: two long tables with benches occupy the sunnier side of the clearing. These serve equally well for eating, crafts, sorting things, and conversation. These were beautifully handmade from the same old fallen tree and have been well cared for over the years.

- **Root Cellar, a minor background feature**: The entrance to this root cellar is carved into a natural bank at the edge of the clearing. The bank rises naturally toward the perimeter of the clearing, and its door is partly nested among roots, moss, and earth. It has a small wooden door which is set into the earth-and-root embankment, making the cellar feel as though it belongs to the Forest instead of becoming another building. The door is currently closed.

  The root cellar must remain small and visually subordinate. It is located at the rear perimeter of the clearing, reached by the narrow path that passes to the right of the Cooking Area and recedes away from the viewer.

  Only the small closed wooden cellar door and a little of its earth-and-root bank should be visible.

  The root cellar must not appear in the foreground, must not sit beside the central stump, must not be built into a huge foreground tree root, and must not become a focal point.

  Its door should appear substantially smaller in the image than the doors/cabinets of the Cooking Area because it is farther from the camera.

- **Gypsa's Hollow connection**: Through a narrow opening between the trees, the viewer can see part of Gypsa's enormous oak in the middle-to-far background, matching the attached Gypsa's Hollow exterior reference. Only part of the trunk and perhaps a short portion of the familiar path should be visible through intervening foliage. This is a visual geographic continuity cue, not a destination marker.

  **Do not add any sign, arrow, text, trail marker, or label pointing toward Gypsa's Hollow.**

  Do not clearly show Gypsa's door, window, porch, garden, watering can, or other domestic details. The tree should be partially obscured by intervening trees and vegetation.

## Rendering Notes

The Workshop Clearing should not look abandoned, ruined, primitive, or makeshift just because it is old. It is ancient by Friendly Forest standards, but lovingly maintained. The shelter roofs have been repaired over generations. Drawer fronts around the stump do not all match perfectly because some are replacements. Many tool handles have been worn smooth. Some benches show scratches, shallow cut marks, and flour stains. The place should feel as though dozens of hands have cared for it for a very long time. Both water pumps and basins should look old, practical, and mechanical, with no modern plumbing fixtures. Despite its adaptations, the Center must unmistakably remain the massive rooted stump of the original oak; its irregular bark perimeter, root flare, age, and natural form remain visible.

Render the scene in the same warm, detailed watercolor style as Acorn Crossing and Gypsa's Hollow: enormous mature deciduous trees, moss, ferns, clover, small woodland flowers, irregular natural paths, handmade structures adapted to the existing landscape, seasonally appropriate natural light, and slightly softer/bluer detail in the distance. Keep the environment itself primary. No characters are present in the canonical background.

Each water station consists of one basin and one separate hand pump. The basin is a simple utility wash basin. The pump should resemble an old cast-iron hand pump used at a well or outdoor wash station, not a household sink faucet.

The shelters and work stations should not look like a matching set built all at once. They should feel related, but gradually added, repaired, and adapted over time. Avoid creating a market-stall or craft-fair appearance. The Workshop Clearing is a real working place, not a display of charming props.

**Mobile-safe core**: The central stump/workbench, recognizable portion of the Cooking Area, and at least part of the Cookie Cutter Cabinet should remain clustered near enough to the center of the full-size composition that a 706 × 941 vertical crop can preserve the identity of the Workshop Clearing.

## Image Composition/Depth Map  
*The entire clearing should feel busy in capability but calm in appearance. Do not display examples of every craft or fill every shelf, peg, table, and counter. Most equipment is stored away. A few carefully chosen objects should imply current or recent activity.*  

The viewer stands near the entrance to the clearing, facing the Cooking Area. Use a moderately close establishing view rather than a distant panoramic view.  

**Foreground**: The massive Center stump/workbench dominates the lower-center of the image. Its stools surround it. The stump is the primary visual anchor.  

**Left Mid-ground**: The Social Area occupies the left side and may extend partly out of frame.  

**Left-Center Mid-ground**: The Cookie Cutter Cabinet stands between the Social Area and Cooking Area.  

**Center Midground**: The Cooking Area is directly behind the stump and remains clearly visible.  

**Right Midground**: Exactly three separate work stations extend from the Cooking Area toward the right side of the image. They may recede slightly in perspective, but all three distinct roofs and platforms must be visible.  

**Left Background**: Through trees beyond the path, show a partial glimpse of Gypsa’s enormous oak. No sign, arrow, text, or marker identifies it.  

**Right Background**: A narrow path beside the Cooking Area recedes toward the rear perimeter and ends at the small closed Root Cellar door. The cellar is a minor background detail, not a foreground feature.  

## Seasons
**Seasonal transformation rule**: The approved Summer image is the immutable structural baseline. Preserve the exact camera position, composition, trees, branches, stump size and shape, paths, shelters, stoves, cabinets, work stations, tables, root cellar location, and all other permanent geometry. Change only the seasonal foliage, weather, lighting, temporary objects, and evidence of current activity described below.  

**Summer**
In summer, the Workshop Clearing is bright and green beneath a full forest canopy. Sunlight filters through the leaves in patches, leaving the work areas pleasantly shaded while the social tables receive more direct light. Ferns, moss, clover, and small woodland flowers grow naturally around the edges of the clearing but do not obstruct paths or work areas. The ground is dry and well used. Only a few tools and project materials are visible; most are stored away. The cook stoves are lit. One of the Cookie Cutter Cabinet's top doors is partially open, showing two large cookie cutters hanging inside.

**Fall**  
In the fall, the Workshop Clearing sees its busiest time of the year.  The leaves on the trees are changing colors and falling.  Residents have harvested their gardens and are using the Workshop Clearing to preserve harvests for the winter.  There are large pots on the stoves, pies in the ovens, and a few fresh jars of jam ready for storing.  Both of the Cookie Cutter Cabinet's doors are fully open.  One cookie cutter is missing from its peg and is now sitting on the table between the two cook stoves next to two smaller cookie cutters, a rolling pin, a mixing bowl, and a canister of flour.  

There's also evidence of preparation for the annual Friendly Forest Harvest Festival.  Over time, the residents have hand crafted decorations, with table cloths and centerpieces covering the long tables of the Social Area.  The Central tree stump has also been cleared and covered with its own mat, a thin quilt, cut and sewn to perfectly cover the top without hanging over.  It has the most important role of all: this is where the feast food will be set.  There's a stack of plates, a pile of neatly rolled napkins, each containing its own silverware, and an assortment of cups.

**Winter**  
In winter, all of the leaves on the trees have fallen.  Residents cleared these leaves from the Workshop Clearing, but now it's snowing, and there's a fresh blanket of snow covering the roofs, the tree branches, and the ground outside the clearing.  The shelves, structurally unchanged throughout the year, are now filled with preserves.  The Cookie Cutter Cabinet is fully closed. All other tools, containers, and supplies have been put away.  The residents have stacked firewood inside the Cooking Area to keep it dry, though the cook stove nearest the sink is still being used.  There's a light dusting of snow covering the Central tree stump and paths, but it's evident that this area was cleared of snow and in use before it started snowing again.  

**Spring**  
In the spring, the trees are just starting to show buds of new leaves.  It's a bright but overcast day.  A gentle rain is falling, and the stepping stones are darkened from being wet.  Many of the preserves are gone from the shelves, as is most of the firewood.  Neither stove is currently being used, and everything has been put away in the Cooking Area.  The Cookie Cutter Cabinet doors are fully closed.  The Root Cellar door is slightly open. The interior remains mostly dark; only the faintest suggestion of stored shelves is visible, without changing the size, position, or shape of the cellar entrance.    There's a rake and a hoe propped up against the Root Cellar on the side opposite the door.  There are also signs of repairs being planned and in progress:  There's a wooden toolbox sitting on the middle workstation table.  There's a small stack of short wooden boards on the Central tree stump.  At the Fine Handwork station, a small wood-carving project, carving tools, and wood shavings indicate current use; the station itself remains unchanged.
