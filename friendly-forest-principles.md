# The Seven Principles of the Friendly Forest  

1. Foundation before finish.  
2. Every curve has a job.  
3. Every relationship has a reason.  
4. Discover before decorating.  
5. Validate before approving.  
6. Build nature first. Reveal character second.  
7. Let personality emerge; don't impose it.  
8. When integrating a new component into an approved structure, freeze the approved structure. Test the component—not the whole system again.  
8b. Integration testing should reveal whether the new component works with the baseline, not give the baseline permission to drift.  
9. Rotation changes not only a component's apparent shape, but also which parts of its construction are allowed to remain visible.
10. When iterative correction begins moving an approved design farther from its baseline, stop optimizing. Roll back to the last stable version and transfer the known-good geometry directly.
11. Once two components are independently approved, integrate them by preserving their approved geometry—not by asking either component to be rediscovered during assembly.

*The Friendly Forest Methodology isn't trying to produce better drawings.  It's trying to produce truer characters.*
